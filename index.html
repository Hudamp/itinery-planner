How to book the two night trains, Tashkent ⇄ Bukhara, which trains to pick, and what to expect.

Night trains: Tashkent → Bukhara (Sat 12 Dec) and Bukhara → Tashkent (Mon 14 Dec)

Book both now: sales for these dates are openTickets go on sale about 45 days ahead. On each train, take all 4 berths of one coupé compartment so it’s just the four of you. Popular trains sell out.

How to book, step by step

  1. Open eticket.railway.uz or the Uzrailway tickets app, and switch the language to EN at the top right.
  2. Search Tashkent → Bukhara on 12.12.2026 and pick the late-evening sleeper in Coupé (4-berth). Choose all 4 berths in the same compartment on the seat map.
  3. Then search Bukhara → Tashkent on 14.12.2026 and do the same with the late-evening sleeper.
  4. Enter each passenger’s passport name, number, nationality, date of birth and gender exactly as in the passport.
  5. Pay by card. If a card fails, try another, or ask your Tashkent hotel to help.
  6. Save the e-tickets offline. Arrive 40 minutes early: there’s a security scan and a passport check, so carry the same passport you booked with.

Will you see scenery from the train?

Not much: both trains run in the dark (sunset ~5 pm, sunrise ~7:45 am). Treat them as moving hotels that save you two nights’ cost. The scenic parts are the drives to and from Samarkand: the Hungry Steppe and Tamerlane’s Gate canyon.

  • Bring snacks, water and a warm layer. Bedding is provided in sleeper classes.
  • Take one small bag each. Leave suitcases at your Tashkent hotel.
  • The Tashkent railway information line is 1005.

Prayer, mosques and halal food

Uzbekistan is mostly Sunni Hanafi Muslim, with active mosques in every city on your route. Each day’s plan above shows that day’s five prayer times and a mosque near your stops. The full table is below.

CityMosques for daily prayer
TashkentHazrati Imam (Khast Imam) Mosque, the city’s main Friday mosque. Minor (White) Mosque by the Ankhor canal. Khoja Akhrar Vali Juma Mosque beside Chorsu Bazaar.
SamarkandHazrat Khizr Mosque, next to Siab Bazaar. The Imam al-Bukhari complex, about 25 km north, is planned for the afternoon of the 16th.
BukharaKalon (Poi-Kalyan) Mosque, the main mosque in the old town. Bolo-Hauz Mosque opposite the Ark. On the 14th you pray all of Dhuhr to Isha in Bukhara before the night train.
On the roadTowns along the M39 (Gulistan, Jizzakh) have mosques. Dhuhr around Jizzakh on the 15th, Maghrib in Jizzakh on the 17th.
  • Times: calculated with the Muslim World League method (Fajr 18°, Isha 17°) and Hanafi Asr, in Uzbekistan time (UTC+5). Local mosque times can differ by a few minutes, so check the mosque board or a prayer app.
  • Juma, Fri 18 Dec: plan to be at Hazrati Imam by about 12:00. Dhuhr is ~12:22, and the khutbah and prayer usually follow over the next hour. Ask your hotel for the exact time.
  • Women: many large mosques have a women’s section and separate wudu areas (Hazrati Imam does), but smaller ones may not, so ask at the door.
  • Qibla: roughly south-west from all three cities. A qibla app on your phone works offline.
  • Halal food: Uzbek dishes (plov, samsa, shashlik, lagman) are normally made with lamb, beef or chicken. Some restaurants serve alcohol, and pork is uncommon. If in doubt, ask “halol?”.
  • Pack: a light travel prayer mat helps on the driving days and the train back from Bukhara.

Driving rules to remember

  • Carry your licence, International Driving Permit, passport and rental papers. Police checkpoints are common.
  • Speed cameras are strict. One renter got four tickets of 412,000 som (about AED 127) each.
  • Zero alcohol. The legal limit is 0.0%.
  • Drive in daylight outside cities where you can. On the evening of the 17th, slow down, use high beams carefully, and watch for unlit vehicles, carts and people. Fill up before long stretches.
  • Photograph and video the car at pickup and drop-off.
