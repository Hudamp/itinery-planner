How to book the night train on 17 Dec, which train to pick, and what you’ll see.

Night train booking: Bukhara → Tashkent, Thu 17 Dec

Book now: sales for 17 Dec are already openTickets usually go on sale about 45 days before departure (some sources say 60), and popular trains sell out.

How to book, step by step

  1. Open eticket.railway.uz or the Uzrailway tickets app, and switch the language to EN at the top right.
  2. Search Bukhara → Tashkent on 17.12.2026. Bukhara’s station (Bukhara-1) is in Kogon, about 15 km from the old town.
  3. Pick a night train and the Coupé (4-berth) class. Choose all 4 berths in the same compartment on the seat map.
  4. Enter each passenger’s passport name, number, nationality, date of birth and gender exactly as in the passport.
  5. Pay by card. The railway lists Mastercard, American Express, Discover, Diners Club and JCB. If your card fails, try another, or ask your Bukhara guesthouse to help.
  6. Save the e-tickets offline. On the night, arrive 30–45 minutes early: there’s a security scan and a passport check, so carry the same passport you booked with.

Which train

Night train ~22:40 → ~06:34Leave after dinner and wake up in Tashkent. Gives you the most time for Friday on the 18th.
Sleeper ~00:05 → ~08:52Later departure and a later arrival. You get about an hour of daylight before Tashkent (sunrise ~07:43).

Will you see scenery from the train?

Not much on this one. The sun sets in Bukhara at about 5:19 pm and rises in Tashkent at about 7:43 am, so the whole ride is in the dark. Treat it as a moving hotel that saves you a night’s cost. If you take the 00:05 sleeper, you’ll get about an hour of morning light over the flat Syrdarya farmland before Tashkent.

The scenic parts of your trip are the drives: the Hungry Steppe and Tamerlane’s Gate canyon on the 13th, the Amankutan gorge on the 14th, and the Zarafshan valley farmland between Samarkand and Bukhara on the 15th.

Prayer, mosques and halal food

Uzbekistan is mostly Sunni Hanafi Muslim, with active mosques in every city on your route. Each day’s plan above shows that day’s five prayer times and a mosque near your stops. The full table is below.

CityMosques for daily prayer
TashkentHazrati Imam (Khast Imam) Mosque, the city’s main Friday mosque. Minor (White) Mosque by the Ankhor canal. Khoja Akhrar Vali Juma Mosque beside Chorsu Bazaar.
SamarkandHazrat Khizr Mosque, next to Siab Bazaar. The Imam al-Bukhari complex, about 25 km north, is an optional visit on the 14th.
BukharaKalon (Poi-Kalyan) Mosque, the main mosque in the old town. Bolo-Hauz Mosque opposite the Ark.
On the roadTowns along the M39 (Gulistan, Jizzakh, Navoi) have mosques. Plan Dhuhr around your Jizzakh or Samarkand stop.
  • Times: calculated with the Muslim World League method (Fajr 18°, Isha 17°) and Hanafi Asr, in Uzbekistan time (UTC+5). Local mosque times can differ by a few minutes, so check the mosque board or a prayer app.
  • Juma, Fri 18 Dec: plan to be at Hazrati Imam by about 12:00. Dhuhr is ~12:22, and the khutbah and prayer usually follow over the next hour. Ask your hotel for the exact time.
  • Women: many large mosques have a women’s section and separate wudu areas (Hazrati Imam does), but smaller ones may not, so ask at the door.
  • Qibla: roughly south-west from all three cities. A qibla app on your phone works offline.
  • Halal food: Uzbek dishes (plov, samsa, shashlik, lagman) are normally made with lamb, beef or chicken. Some restaurants serve alcohol, and pork is uncommon. If in doubt, ask “halol?”.
  • Pack: a light travel prayer mat helps for Dhuhr and Asr on the long driving days.

Driving rules to remember

  • Carry your licence, International Driving Permit, passport and rental papers. Police checkpoints are common.
  • Speed cameras are strict. One renter got four tickets of 412,000 som (about AED 127) each.
  • Zero alcohol. The legal limit is 0.0%.
  • Drive only in daylight outside cities. Fill up before long stretches.
  • Photograph and video the car at pickup and drop-off.
